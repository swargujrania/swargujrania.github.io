DIY Drone kit

Interacting with AR Parrot 2.0 through mobile interface

Summary


You got a new drone! A shiny quad-copter, complete with a remote. You put thebatteries in the remote controller and start flying. As you fly, you realize that thedrone can go only a little distance, unless you are following it. That’s a littleannoying! Your quad-copter has very limited range!

This drone kit was designed as quick DIY toolkit to get one’s AR ParrotDrone 2.0 to interface with, and be controlled by, their mobile devices.The communication happened over cellular data, thereby removing theneed for expensive WiFi beacons required to facilitate, and communicatewith, the drone

Problem Space


A number of commercial drones, meant for non-businessusage, have very limited range of communication. They oftentalk to the remote controller over WiFi channels.

For this, drones need to have an in-built WiFi modulethat can connect with other WiFi enabled devices toreceive commands for control.

Hello!??

Can’t hear you..

Given the battery constraints, however, the drone’s WiFimodule lacks significant range. As a result, you have twooptions if you want your drone to go afar:

Walk with the drone

This simple solution becomes challenging since it’s difficult to keep up with thespeed of the drone, while managing the controls like pitch and yaw of the drone.As a result, your attention gets distributed and you focus more on controllingand keeping up with the drone, instead of having fun with it.

Setup WiFi beacons

You plan the drone path ahead of time, and setup WiFi beacons so that they canact as communication relay for your drone. This method is extremely expensive,not to mention tedious and highly constrained.

Imagine setting up beacons everytimeyou want to fly the drone! It kinda takesthe fun out of it. And unless you’re adeptat dealing with wifi beacons and othersimilar devices, it may be really difficultfor you

CONSTRAINED

Every time you want to fly the drone tolarger distances, you have to know thearea at least, if not the path. Further, notall terrains will be suitable to setup theWiFi beacons

TEDIOUS

Even if you don’t mind the constraints and tediousness, theprice of the beacons will not be exactly cheap, not tomention the additional setup cost. And sensors and smallcommunication devices often have high failure rate in thewild (uncontrolled) environments

So what if..


In this problem, there are two areas where we could intervene:

Enhancing the WiFi reception of the drone.This is difficult given the proprietary natureof the device we were working with.

Replace the need of WiFi beacon withsomething more ubiquitous.

While people don’t carry wifi beacons around with them, there is one communication devicewhich almost everyone in an urbane scenario has - a mobile phone. We decided to figure a wayout for using mobile devices as remote controllers.

Challenges


This project was envisioned particularly around providing people the ability to live-stream events at theirconvenience. Given the numerous technical challenges and high-level nature of the problem, it had to be brokendown into simpler, more low-level problems, out of which, we chose the problem of restriction on communicationrange to work on.

We also faced some issues around the lag in communication with the drone. Forinstance, in the starting, it took the drone ~ 5 sec to receive a command andexecute it. For this, we had to redesign our architecture and re-structure thecomponents that we were using. We were able to bring the performance downto < 1 second.

We also aimed to provide more ‘intelligence’ to the drone, by building thecapabilities of obstacle detection and avoidance. We designed the system intheory, however, due to some constraints on time and resources, it couldn’t bematerialized completely.
