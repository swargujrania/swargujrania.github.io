Measuring flow has a diverse set of applications, ranging from understanding productivity to why people self-improve.
Traditionally, flow has been measured through questionnairs, which is slow, intrusive and can only be done when the person is out
of the flow inducing activity that researchers are testing her for (Catch 22 much!)

Thus, in our study, we augment the standard questionnaires with physiological measures and examine them against player’s
performance to see if there are any patterns.

Experiment design for the project

The game was designed to provide ascending levels of difficulty with 3 natural breakpoints, in which participants took a shortsurvey about their immediate experience. At the end of the session, participants a different kind of questionnaire to assess theirnatural tendency for flow in video games.

Game Design

Onslaught Arena is a simple arcade shooter game with minimal controls. The game levels
allow us to understand how participants with different skill levels engage with the game
at different difficult levels. It also provides useful statistics, such as player accuracy and
overall score which helped us learn more about a players’ relative skills.

Self-reported Questionnaire

We use two kinds of questionnaires to assess how participants felt about their own state of Flow:1. Flow State Scale (FSS): Used in between the sessions to measures flow with respect to certain instance of an activity.2. Dispositional Flow Scale (DFS 2): Used as the final questionnaire to measures one’s tendency to experience Flow, in a particular
domain.

Calculating Flow

Based on previous works, and focusing on the factors of Flow that are to be measured during a video game experience, we crafted
the following questions:

FactorQuestionComments
Concentration during gameplay (F1)How concentrated were you while playing?This is a measure of how engaged the user was while playing each level of the game. This is a self-reported measure and hence inherently subjective.
Perceived difficulty during gameplay (F2)What amount of challenge did you feel while playing?Csikszentmihalyi et al. [17] observed perceived challenge as a strong predictor of player enjoyment in chess games assuming intrinsic motivation of the players. The players enjoyed more when playing against other players of slightly high chess rating which lead to close games
Concentration enjoyment during gameplay (F3)Did you enjoy the concentration level that you had during the game?

Did you feel stressed while playing?		In their recent paper [16], Corcos et al. have argued that perceived game enjoyment improves the estimation of Flow, and can even give better results than the traditional measures namely concentration and enjoyment. They used concentration enjoyment and challenge enjoyment to measure Flow in different levels of FPS (first-person-shooting) games, and presented data-based evidence of their hypothesis.
Challenge enjoyment during gameplay (F4)What amount of challenge did you feel while playing?


Do you enjoy the challenge that the game poses?		This factor indicates how much did the players enjoy the challenge that they experienced in different levels of the game. Like F3, it emphasizes the experience of the player rather than just concentration and perceived challenge.
Perceived skill during gameplay (F5)How comfortable did you feel with the increasing no. of monsters?

How much control did you feel while playing the game?		One problem with the enjoyment related measures (F3, F4) is that they can be biased by the player’s affect post completing the level (like through achieved scores) etc. Players’ perceived skill during the game or how well did he play the game aims to balance that bias. Hence, high level of F3, F4 along with high F5 would indicate bias on post-game affect and can be compensated for in the analysis. Otherwise, an intermediate level response on F5 would indicate Flow.

ANALYSIS & CONCLUSION


We analyzed the gaze and position data using K-Means clustering. Both player position and gaze position significantly depends on
the player’s style and skill level.

We observed that:1. More experienced players had significantly more movement thanless experienced ones.

2. Our chosen game did not restrict gaze patterns because of
random nature on moster attacks.

For Affect, there were mostly no general trends. However, for experienced gamers (P4 and P11), whose Flow score increased with
each session, the mean and variance in valence and engagement followed identical trends:

However, while these two participants showed very strong similarities, this did not hold true for the rest of the less proficient
participant pool making these results statistically insignificant.

Conclusion

There could be several explanation for these results:

1. Physiological measures are not an accurate indicator

of Flow

and ill-suited for the task.

2. The measures we chose were too noisy

3. People express emotions

differently.

While there are some observable correlations, a strong relationship between our physiological measures and the calculated Flow
score could not be found. However, we believe there is enough evidence to warrant further study in the relationship between Flow
and physiological measures.
